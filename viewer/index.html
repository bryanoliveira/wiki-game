How the numbers are computed

Cost to go
The fewest steps from the current state to the target. In the clicks model the player sees the whole page and each click costs 1, so the cost to go is the shortest-path length d in the link graph. In the actions model the player sees one chunk of about 2,000 characters, and each click or scroll costs 1. The state is then (page, chunk) and the cost to go is V.
Regret of a step
1 + cost to go after − cost to go before. It is 0 for an optimal step. Over a finished game the regrets add up to steps taken − optimal steps.
Bits to go, φ
Take a player who picks uniformly at random among the available actions. Let P be the probability that it follows an optimal path from here. Then φ = −log₂ P, computed exactly by P(x) = (1/n)·Σ P(next) over the optimal actions. It is the information a player must supply to play optimally from here.
Bits gained by a step
φ before − φ after. With k optimal actions out of n, a random pick needs log₂(n/k) bits (the "chance bits"). Along any game that reaches the target the gains add up to φ(start), so the total is fixed by the task.
Regret and bits measure different things
Regret counts wasted steps. Bits count how much easier the remaining choice became. A step that moves farther from the target loses bits. A step that keeps the same distance can still gain bits when it lands on a page with many good links, such as a hub like Europe. People often play this way: the detour costs a step but makes the rest of the game surer. So a game can have steps with regret and lose no bits.
Efficiency
Optimal steps divided by steps taken, for a game that reached the target (1 is perfect). A game that did not reach it scores 0.
